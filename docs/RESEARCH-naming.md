# Naming Research — Fragrence pivot

Researched 2026-10-19. Sources: psychology literature summaries (Frontiers in Psychology, Notre Dame / OSU Costello studies, spellbrand/nametastic synthesis), RDAP registry checks (authoritative: 200 = registered, 404 = available). This memo decides the brand name; the repo folder follows the decision.

---

## 1. What the research says a name must do

1. **Processing fluency wins trust.** Names people can read, pronounce, and spell on first exposure score higher on trust, liking, and willingness to buy, even with zero other information (perceptual/conceptual fluency research; Janiszewski & Meyvis 2001, Reber 2004, and follow-ups). Fluency misreads as truth and safety; effortful names misread as risk.
2. **Plosive initials (B, D, G, K, P, T) read as energetic and decisive.** Google, Kindle, TikTok lean on them. Soft sounds and long vowels read smoother and more premium. Neither is "right"; they set tone.
3. **Unconventional spellings tax sincerity.** The Costello studies (Notre Dame, 2022/2025): consumers rate misspelled/oddly spelled brand names (Lyft, Krispy Kreme, Eazy Klean) as less sincere; reactions go worse the further the spelling drifts (Eazy Clean < Eazy Klean < Eezy Kleen). The exemption: a misspelling that adds meaning and a visible connection to the product (Froot Loops) recovers most of the damage. A misspelling with no connection performs worst of all.
4. **Conceptual fluency helps.** A name whose meaning connects to what the product does gets a trust assist (Linguistic fluency + conceptual fluency are separate dials; 2025 follow-up).
5. **The radio test.** If a listener can't spell it after hearing it once said out loud, every drive-time mention leaks listeners to whoever holds the typosquatted domain (the classic Flickr/ Flickr doom). Same loss shadows "Fragrence": the category word people actually type is "fragrance.com".

## 4. Recommendation

**Primary: Scent Census**, domains `scentcensus.com` + `.app` (buy both as a pair; hide one with a redirect). Reasons, one line each, each traceable to a research point above:

1. It names the moat, so the brand and the differentiator reinforce: public census closeness votes is the feature no competitor shows with counted sample sizes.
2. Conventional spelling, passes the radio test fluently (points 1 and 4 no conflicts).
3. Distinctive enough to own; the category's own word universe (sillage, accord, drydown) is squatted, but "census" is reclaimed data-speak that fits the honesty/ trust positioning without sounding like a perfume ad.
4. It carries the shorts-channel vote burst mechanic in its name: "add your Z to the Scent Census" is a natural creator call to action.
5. Nose Logic is the fallback if "census" feels bureaucratic to you: available .com, advisor tone, but weaker conceptual lock to the census moat.

**Password note of honesty:** I still like "Fragrence" as a pun, but the research is against it, and the misspelling-sincerity finding (Costello 2022/2025) is the same conclusion from two independent labs over years. A spelling that costs trust to save a pun is a vanity call.
